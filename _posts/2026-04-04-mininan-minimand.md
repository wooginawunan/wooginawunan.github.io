We live in a world where information is constantly thrown at us — especially now, with AI. And for me, even more so: *I work on AI, I think fast, I get pulled in many directions — very ADHD, haha*.

Thoughts arrive quickly. Projects overlap. Personal questions blur into work ideas, plans, reminders, and half-formed intuitions.

AI helps us produce more — more chat, more research, more deep research, more multi-agent workflows — ***but not necessarily more understanding.***

And I keep craving the opposite:

- **minimalism**
- **clarity**
- **control**

That is where **MiniMand** comes from — a visual layer for my mind and my life.

Instead of flattening everything into lists, folders, and disconnected notes, I need to step back and see:

- what belongs together  
- what keeps coming back  
- what is quietly growing into something bigger  
- how different parts of my mind and life connect  

Not just as information —  but as **shape**, as **patterns**, as **landscape**.

> **What I want is clarity — in thought, in priorities, in the inner landscape behind them.**

**A Simpler Way to Think About It**

You start with one thought — or many — and place them on the map.

Then you let them grow. Add related ideas, questions, references, and next steps around them. Over time, they branch, cluster, and connect.

Later, you notice it connects to something else in your life — a creative direction, a research question, a relationship pattern, a personal goal.

Gradually, you are no longer looking at isolated notes, lists, or fragments.

You are looking at the **landscape of your mind**.

That is what MiniMand is for -- helping you see and track how your ideas, projects, and life threads connect.

And when you want more detail, you can link a node to Apple Notes.

Shape your thinking in MiniMand. Keep the details in Apple Notes — or anywhere else.

Download MiniMand here: [MiniMand-v0.1.zip](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Bc8RcglljX9qH_gCgs3Lo2ZgKcVbQkHO/view?usp=share_link) *(restricted access; permission request required)*.

### Install

1. Unzip the file.
2. Drag `MiniMand.app` to `/Applications`.
3. Right-click `MiniMand.app` -> **Open** (first launch only, to bypass Gatekeeper).

If macOS blocks MiniMand because it is not from an identified developer, use the steps below:

1. Click **Done** to dismiss the dialog.
2. Go to **System Settings > Privacy & Security**.
3. Scroll down - you will see a message like **"MiniMand" was blocked from use because it is not from an identified developer**.
4. Click **Open Anyway**.
5. It will ask one more time - click **Open**.

## How It Works

MiniMand is a canvas of **nodes** — either **Notes** (individual thoughts) or **Threads** (hubs that anchor a cluster) - connected with **edges**. Each node carries a title, a color-coded tag, and a status.

The layout is force-directed: clusters self-organize and the map settles into a natural shape. Drag nodes freely, **pin** them to anchor your layout, or let physics handle the rest.

Link any node to an Apple Note — or a folder, for threads -- based on title. Links use a stable internal ID, so they survive renames. One click opens the note directly.

MiniMand is local-first. Your graph lives in a simple portable file with automatic backups, and can sync across Macs through iCloud Drive.


## A Final Note

MiniMand is meant to fit into real life, not force you into a new system.

It is intentionally light — flexible, open, and easy to shape in your own way.

It does not try to replace everything else. It does not ask you to move your whole life into yet another tool.

Use it for anything — a thought that appeared in your mind, a question that keeps returning, something unfolding in life.

And let me know how it feels.
